# Results

## Campaign overview and exploratory data analysis

In total, 23,960 measured queries were answered during the campaign. They consumed 2,969 kJ of module energy over 23.1 hours of wall-clock time. The main grid contributed 18,400 queries (23 configurations × 800 items), the power-mode sweep 4,160 queries, and the thinking study 1,400 queries. All 24 main-grid configurations were completed. Two of them (Qwen2.5-3B Q6_K and Q8_0) were quarantined by the idle-machine guard during a foreign training job and were re-run from scratch. The parse-failure rate over the main grid was 0.13%.

A power trace of one minute is shown in [fig:trace]. During decoding, the GPU rail draws about 20 W, the CPU rail 2–3 W, the 5 V system rail 8–10 W, and the memory rail 2–3 W. Between requests, the module drops to its loaded-idle level within about half a second. The idle module power without a model was 9.6 W at MAXN and 9.7 W at 40 W ([fig:idle]). The GPU rail accounts for 3.4 W of this idle power and the 5 V system rail for 4.9 W. Idle power therefore forms a floor of about 10 W that every query pays for its full duration.

{{FIG:figures/fig02_power_trace_example.png|One minute of the module power trace during GSM8K decoding (stacked rails, top) and the GPU load (bottom). The gap near 27 s is the pause between two requests|trace}}

{{FIG:figures/fig03_idle_power_by_mode.png|Idle module power by rail with no model loaded, for the two evaluated power modes|idle}}

The workload characteristics are shown in [fig:tokens]. ARC prompts had a mean length of 110 tokens and BoolQ prompts 190 tokens (Qwen tokenizer), with a long BoolQ tail up to about 1,300 tokens. GSM8K prompts had a mean of 109 tokens, but the outputs had a mean of 195 to 339 tokens depending on the model, with an interquartile range of roughly 220 to 350 tokens. ARC and BoolQ outputs were 2 to 7 tokens. The three tasks therefore span two orders of magnitude in output length, as intended.

{{FIG:figures/fig04_token_distributions.png|Prompt-length distribution per task (left) and GSM8K output-length distribution per model configuration (right, boxes show quartiles and whiskers the full range)|tokens}}

The per-query energy distributions of four representative configurations are shown in [fig:ecdf]. On ARC and BoolQ, the distributions are narrow and separated by model size. The 0.5B model on ARC is bimodal: about one fifth of its answers stopped after two tokens, and the rest ran into the 8-token budget. On GSM8K, the distributions are wide, because energy follows the output length. The 10th-to-90th percentile range of a single configuration spans about a factor of three.

{{FIG:figures/fig05_energy_ecdf.png|Empirical cumulative distributions of per-query energy for four configurations on the three tasks (log scale)|ecdf}}

Output truncation at the ARC budget of 8 tokens affected mainly the smallest Qwen2.5 models. Qwen2.5-0.5B Q4_K_M ran into the budget on 73% of ARC items and the three Qwen2.5-1.5B variants on 47–50%. These models tend to write "The correct answer is B" rather than "B". Because the letter is produced within the first tokens, the parse-failure rate of these configurations stayed at or below 3%, and the scored answers were not changed. The other 17 configurations truncated on fewer than 9% of ARC items ([tab:main] and [tab:latency]). The thermal state stayed stable throughout the campaign. The longest single run was 61 minutes of GSM8K on Qwen2.5-7B Q8_0 ([fig:thermal]). During it, the junction temperature rose from 57.8 °C to 59.5 °C with a maximum of 62.7 °C. The GPU clock stayed at its 930.75 MHz cap during every request. Thermal throttling therefore did not occur.

{{FIG:figures/fig12_thermal_sustained.png|Junction, GPU, and CPU temperatures (left) and GPU clock (right) during the longest single run of the campaign (Qwen2.5-7B Q8_0 on GSM8K at MAXN). The periodic dips are the pauses between requests|thermal}}

## RQ1: Model size and precision

The main-grid results are summarized in [tab:main], and the corresponding latency and throughput figures in [tab:latency]. Accuracy ranged from 28.7% to 90.3% on ARC, from 38.3% to 85.3% on BoolQ, and from 42.5% to 92.5% on GSM8K. Mean energy per query ranged from 1.3 J to 8.6 J on ARC, from 1.6 J to 10.1 J on BoolQ, and from 67.5 J to 731.3 J on GSM8K. Joules per correct answer (JPCA) ranged from 2.8 J to 11.5 J on ARC, from 2.6 J to 12.1 J on BoolQ, and from 145 J to 791 J on GSM8K. The tasks therefore differ by a factor of about 60 in the energy of a correct answer, which is driven by output length.

{{TABLE:paper/tables/T3b_latency.md|Main grid at MAXN: mean end-to-end latency and decode throughput per task, time-to-first-token on ARC, and the truncation and parse-failure rates over all tasks|latency}}

The accuracy–energy plane of every configuration is shown in [fig:pareto], with the Pareto front marked. On ARC, the front consists of Qwen2.5-0.5B Q8_0, Qwen2.5-3B Q4_K_M, Q8_0, and FP16, and Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M. On BoolQ, it consists of the Qwen2.5-0.5B and 1.5B models, Llama 3.2-3B Q8_0, and Gemma 2-2B Q4_K_M and Q8_0. On GSM8K, it consists of eleven configurations from 0.5B to 7B, mostly at Q4_K_M and Q8_0. Three observations follow. First, no FP16 configuration appears on the GSM8K front, and FP16 appears on the ARC front only once (Qwen2.5-3B, with the same accuracy as Q8_0 within 0.3 pp). Second, the Llama 3.2-1B models are dominated on every task: they are as cheap as Qwen2.5-0.5B but far less accurate (28.7–30.3% on ARC against 46.3–48.7%). Third, the lowest JPCA on every task is reached by a 0.5B model, but at an accuracy that would be unacceptable for most uses (48.7% on ARC, 62.7% on BoolQ, and 46.5% on GSM8K). Among configurations with at least 80% accuracy, the lowest JPCA was reached by Qwen2.5-3B Q4_K_M on ARC (4.3 J at 81.3%) and on GSM8K (405 J at 82.0%). On BoolQ, it was reached by Gemma 2-2B Q4_K_M (9.1 J at 85.0%).

{{FIG:figures/fig06_pareto_accuracy_energy.png|Accuracy against mean energy per query for all main-grid configurations at MAXN. Marker color gives the model family, marker shape the quantization level, and marker size the parameter count. The dashed line joins the Pareto-optimal configurations|pareto}}

The pre-specified paired comparisons are listed in [tab:tests]. Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M was more accurate than Qwen2.5-3B FP16 on all three tasks, by 6.3 to 10.3 pp, and the differences on BoolQ and GSM8K survived the Holm correction. The 7B model has a smaller weight file than the 3B FP16 model (4.7 GB against 6.2 GB). It cost 21–34% more energy per correct answer on the short tasks but 9% less on GSM8K, because its higher accuracy outweighed its higher per-query energy. None of the Q4_K_M-versus-FP16 comparisons within a model showed a significant accuracy difference after correction, while Q4_K_M reduced JPCA by 18% to 50%. The largest JPCA reductions were on GSM8K (44% for Qwen2.5-3B, 50% for Qwen2.5-1.5B, 43% for Llama 3.2-3B, and 34% for Gemma 2-2B), where decoding dominates. The Q4_K_M-versus-Q8_0 comparisons likewise showed no significant accuracy difference, with JPCA reductions of 3% to 17%.

{{TABLE:paper/tables/T7_tests.md|Pre-specified paired comparisons on identical items: accuracy difference with its paired-bootstrap 95% confidence interval, the discordant item counts (A correct and B wrong / A wrong and B correct) on which the exact McNemar test is based, the exact and Holm-corrected p-values, and the JPCA ratio with its paired-bootstrap 95% confidence interval|tests}}

The relation between energy and model size is shown in [fig:law]. Decode energy per output token increased linearly with the weight-file size: E/token = 0.350 J + 0.255 J/GB × size, with R² = 0.885 over 23 configurations. The slope corresponds to an effective weight-streaming bandwidth of about 164 GB/s, which is close to the 204.8 GB/s peak of the module's LPDDR5 memory. The intercept of 0.35 J per token is the fixed cost of generating a token regardless of model size. Time per output token followed the same linear law with R² = 0.805. The log-linear regression of per-query energy on model size, output length, and prompt length ([tab:regression]) explained 98.4% of the variance over 18,398 queries. The elasticity of energy with respect to output length was 0.97, with respect to weight-file size 0.69, and with respect to prompt length 0.47. In other words, doubling the output almost doubles the energy, whereas doubling the model size adds about 60%. Llama 3.2 and Qwen2.5 configurations used 24% and 13% less energy than Gemma 2 at equal size and lengths.

## RQ2: Power modes

The effect of the 40 W mode relative to MAXN is shown in [fig:modes] and summarized in [tab:modes]. The outputs were identical in the two modes: all 2,080 responses of the eight sweep configurations matched token for token, and so did their correctness. This confirms that greedy decoding makes the model answers independent of the power mode, and it justifies the reuse of the MAXN accuracy estimates. In the 40 W mode, energy per query fell by 1.5% on ARC, 1.4% on BoolQ, and 3.8% on GSM8K (medians over the eight configurations), while latency rose by 10% on every task. The energy–delay product rose by 6% to 9%. Mean power during GSM8K decoding fell from 35.7 W to 31.3 W. The joules per correct answer followed the energy, because accuracy was unchanged. The lower budget therefore bought a small energy saving at a proportionally larger latency cost. This is consistent with the race-to-idle argument: the idle floor of about 10 W is paid for the whole, longer, duration.

{{FIG:figures/fig09_power_modes.png|Per-item ratio of energy per query (top) and latency per query (bottom) between the 40 W mode and MAXN for the eight sweep configurations and three tasks. Boxes show the quartiles over items and whiskers the 1.5 IQR range|modes}}

{{TABLE:paper/tables/T4_modes.md|Power-mode sweep: mean power, energy per query, and the median ratios of energy, latency, and JPCA relative to MAXN over the eight configurations (100 ARC, 100 BoolQ, and 60 GSM8K items)|modes}}

The repetition of the eight sweep configurations at MAXN also provided a test–retest estimate. Across 2,080 overlapping queries measured in two separate passes, the median absolute relative difference in per-query energy was 2.3%, and the correlation of log energy between passes was 0.9995. At the level of configuration totals, the mean difference was 0.3% and the largest 9.4%. The earlier repetition of Qwen2.5-1.5B FP16 after 17 days gave the same picture: identical predictions on 600 items and a mean energy difference of 2.2–2.3%. The energy measurements are therefore repeatable to within a few percent, which is small compared with the differences between configurations.

## RQ3: Quantization ladder

The six-level ladder of Qwen2.5-3B is shown in [fig:ladder]. Accuracy rose steeply from Q3_K_M to Q4_K_M: by 15.3 pp on ARC, 8.3 pp on BoolQ, and 30.5 pp on GSM8K, all significant after correction ([tab:tests]). Above Q4_K_M, accuracy was flat within the confidence intervals: on ARC 81.3%, 83.3%, 83.3%, 83.7%, and 84.0% for Q4_K_M, Q5_K_M, Q6_K, Q8_0, and FP16, and on GSM8K 82.0%, 84.0%, 84.5%, 85.0%, and 83.0%. On BoolQ, Q4_K_M was the most accurate level. Energy, by contrast, kept rising with the bits per weight. On GSM8K, JPCA went from 405 J at Q4_K_M to 439 J at Q5_K_M, 516 J at Q6_K, 479 J at Q8_0, and 723 J at FP16. Q3_K_M was worse on both axes: its accuracy was the lowest, and its decode throughput (27 tok/s) was lower than that of Q4_K_M (36 tok/s), because the 3-bit kernel is slower on this GPU. Q6_K was likewise slower than Q8_0 (28.5 against 29.3 tok/s) despite its smaller file. The ladder therefore has a knee at Q4_K_M: below it, accuracy collapses; above it, only energy grows.

## RQ4: Thinking on and off

The results of the thinking study are given in [tab:thinking] and [fig:think]. For Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-8B, the same hybrid model was run with the reasoning phase switched on and off. For the 4B size, the Ollama tag `qwen3:4b` resolves to Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, a model that reasons unconditionally. With the switch set to off, it still wrote its reasoning into the answer field and was cut by the output budget. This gave 1% accuracy on ARC and 18% on GSM8K, with 97–100% truncation. This run was excluded, and the non-thinking sibling Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 (same Q4_K_M precision) was measured as the off arm instead. The 4B pair therefore compares two post-training variants of one base model rather than one switch.

{{TABLE:paper/tables/T5_thinking.md|Thinking study on 100 ARC and 100 GSM8K items: accuracy, mean output tokens, latency, energy per query, JPCA, and the share of outputs cut at the budget|thinking}}

Thinking multiplied the output length by 80 to 330 on ARC and by 3 to 6 on GSM8K. Energy per query followed. On ARC, it rose from 6 J to 509 J for 1.7B (×82), from 8 J to 1,896 J for 4B (×225), and from 13 J to 1,225 J for 8B (×93). On GSM8K it rose from 188 J to 1,228 J for 1.7B (×6.5), from 432 J to 2,010 J for 4B (×4.7), and from 582 J to 3,437 J for 8B (×5.9). The accuracy gains did not justify these costs in most cases. For 1.7B, thinking *lowered* ARC accuracy from 70% to 58% (exact McNemar test on the discordant items: 19 items were correct only with thinking and 31 only without, p = 0.12) and raised GSM8K accuracy from 79% to 84% (10 versus 5 discordant items, p = 0.30). For 8B, thinking raised ARC accuracy from 93% to 96% (3 versus 0 discordant items, p = 0.25) and lowered GSM8K accuracy from 94% to 92% (2 versus 4 discordant items, p = 0.69). None of these differences was significant on 100 items. For 4B, the thinking variant reached 92% on ARC and 90% on GSM8K. The instruct variant reached 91% (3 versus 2 discordant items, p = 1.00) and 86% (6 versus 2 discordant items, p = 0.29) at 1/225 and 1/4.7 of the energy. Expressed per joule, the cost of thinking was large: JPCA rose from 8.9 J to 877 J for 1.7B on ARC and from 619 J to 3,735 J for 8B on GSM8K. Where thinking did raise accuracy, the marginal cost was 20.8 kJ per extra correct GSM8K answer for 1.7B and 40.4 kJ per extra correct ARC answer for 8B. On GSM8K, 5–11% of the thinking outputs were cut at the 4,096-token budget, so some of the energy was spent on answers that were never completed.

## RQ5: Agreement cascade

The cascade results on the held-out halves are given in [tab:cascade] and [fig:cascade]. On every task, the cascade that was selected on the selection half was dominated on the held-out half by the best single model. On ARC, the selected cascade (Llama 3.2-3B Q4_K_M and Qwen2.5-3B Q4_K_M, escalating to Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M) reached 90.6% at 9.0 J per query, whereas Qwen2.5-7B Q8_0 alone reached 92.6% at 7.1 J. On BoolQ, the cascade reached 84.7% at 12.1 J against 85.3% at 9.9 J for Gemma 2-2B FP16 alone. On GSM8K, the cascade reached 90.0% at 694 J against 90.0% at 623 J for Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M alone. The cascades therefore cost 11% to 26% more energy than the best single model at the same or lower accuracy (exact McNemar tests on the held-out items: p = 0.38, 1.00, and 1.00). The reason is visible in the escalation rates: 28% of ARC items, 34% of BoolQ items, and 56% of GSM8K items were escalated. When two small models disagree often, the energy of both small models is added to the energy of the large model, and little is saved. The oracle router shows the headroom that a perfect confidence signal would offer. It reached 98% to 99% accuracy at 2.2 J, 2.3 J, and 131 J per query on the three tasks. This is three to five times less energy than the best single model. Agreement between two small models is therefore too weak a signal to approach this bound.
