# Discussion

## Practical guidance

The results can be turned into four rules for an operator who deploys a small language model on a Jetson-class device.

- BULLET: **Choose the model by the accuracy floor, then minimize JPCA.** Energy per token rewards the smallest model, but the smallest models answer less than two thirds of the questions correctly. Once a floor is set, the cheapest correct answer in this study came from a 2B to 3B model at Q4_K_M for an 80% floor, and from Qwen2.5-7B Q4_K_M for a 90% floor. At the 90% floor, the 7B model at Q4_K_M was both more accurate and cheaper per correct GSM8K answer than any 3B variant, and it fit in 4.7 GB.
- BULLET: **Use Q4_K_M unless accuracy at Q4_K_M is shown to be insufficient.** Across six model-level comparisons, Q4_K_M lost no significant accuracy against Q8_0 or FP16. It saved 18% to 50% of the energy per correct answer. Q3_K_M should be avoided: it lost 8 to 31 points of accuracy and was also slower than Q4_K_M, because the 3-bit kernel is less efficient on this GPU. Q6_K was slower than Q8_0 for the same reason.
- BULLET: **Do not expect the power mode to save energy per answer.** The 40 W mode saved 1% to 4% of energy and cost 10% of latency. Power modes are useful to meet a thermal or supply limit, not to reduce the energy of a fixed workload. If the workload is bursty, the same energy can be saved by finishing fast and returning to idle.
- BULLET: **Switch thinking off for short-answer and grade-school arithmetic tasks.** On this device, a reasoning phase multiplied the energy per query by 3 to 225. It gave no significant accuracy gain for the hybrid 1.7B and 8B models on the two tasks studied. Thinking belongs to tasks where the non-thinking accuracy is clearly insufficient, and then with an output budget that is large enough to let the reasoning finish.

## Comparison with previous studies

The findings agree with, and extend, earlier measurements. The near-lossless behavior of 4-bit weights for recent instruction-tuned models matches the broad evaluations of Li et al. [@li2024evaluating] and Jin et al. [@jin2024comprehensive], and the collapse at 3 bits matches the degradation that Huang et al. reported for LLaMA3 at ultra-low bit widths [@huang2024llama3quant]. The linear relation between energy per token and weight-file size is the energy counterpart of the memory-bound decoding that Laskaridis et al. observed with MELT [@laskaridis2024melting]. The observation that Q3_K_M and Q6_K run slower than larger formats agrees with Arya et al., who found that quantization can make smaller LLMs slower on the AGX Orin [@arya2025understanding]. On a Raspberry Pi 4, Husom et al. found that quantization trades accuracy against energy across 28 Ollama models [@husom2025sustainable]. The present study adds the GPU side of that picture, a per-correct-answer normalization, and paired tests on identical items. The weak effect of the 40 W mode is consistent with the race-to-idle argument for processors [@lesueur2010dynamic; @awan2014race] and with the finding of Xu et al. that GPU frequency alone buys limited energy savings on the AGX Orin [@xu2025camel]. The high energy price of thinking echoes the overthinking phenomenon documented by Chen et al. [@chen2025overthinking] and the accuracy–latency frontier of EdgeReasoning [@kubwimana2025edgereasoning], now expressed in measured joules. Finally, the result that output length drives energy with an elasticity near one agrees with Jin et al., who found output length to be the main driver of the energy of test-time compute on datacenter GPUs [@jin2025energy].

## Why the results look the way they do

Three mechanisms explain most of the observed structure. The first is memory-bound decoding. Each output token requires the full weight file to be streamed from LPDDR5 memory, so the energy and time per token grew linearly with the file size ([fig:law]). The fitted effective bandwidth of about 164 GB/s is 80% of the 204.8 GB/s peak of the module. Quantization helps because it shrinks the file, not because it reduces arithmetic, and a quantization format whose kernel is slow (Q3_K_M, Q6_K) can cost more than a larger but faster one.

The second mechanism is the fixed cost per token and per query. The intercept of 0.35 J per output token and the idle floor of about 10 W set a price that does not depend on the model. On ARC and BoolQ, only a few tokens are generated, so the prefill and the fixed overheads dominate. There, the difference between a 0.5B and a 7B model was only a factor of five in energy. On GSM8K, where hundreds of tokens are generated, the same two models differed by a factor of ten. This is also why the 40 W mode did not help. The dynamic power of the GPU fell, but the fixed power was paid for 10% longer.

The third mechanism is token blow-up. Thinking increased output length by two orders of magnitude on ARC, and chain-of-thought on GSM8K already produces several hundred tokens. Because the elasticity of energy with respect to output length is close to one, any method that adds tokens adds energy almost proportionally. A method must therefore raise accuracy by more than it raises length to improve JPCA, and in this study neither thinking nor the agreement cascade did so. The cascade failed for a related reason: two small models disagreed on 28% to 56% of the items, and every disagreement added the energy of three models instead of one.

## Limitations and threats to validity

Several limitations should be kept in mind when the results are read. First, a single device was used. The AGX Orin 32 GB is one point in the Jetson family, and the absolute numbers will differ on an Orin Nano, an Orin NX, or a newer Thor module. The *shape* of the trade-offs is expected to transfer, because the same memory-bound decoding and the same DVFS mechanisms are shared by these devices, but this was not tested here.

Second, module power was read from the on-board INA3221 sensors, and no calibrated external meter was available. A systematic bias of these sensors against an external reference has been reported [@shalavi2023accurate]. Absolute joule values should therefore be read as module-level estimates. All comparisons in this paper are made between configurations that were measured with the same sensors on the same device, and a consistent multiplicative bias is canceled in such ratios. The carrier board, the fan, and the peripherals are not covered by the sensors, so system-level energy is higher than the reported module-level energy.

Third, each item was answered once per configuration with greedy decoding. Greedy decoding removes sampling noise and makes the outputs reproducible, but it may understate the accuracy that sampling-based methods such as self-consistency could reach [@wang2023selfconsistency]. The test–retest checks that were reported show that the energy of a repeated run was within a few percent, so a single run per item was judged sufficient for the energy comparisons.

Fourth, the benchmark subsets were drawn once with a fixed seed (300 ARC, 300 BoolQ, and 200 GSM8K items for the main grid, and fewer for the power-mode sweep). The bootstrap confidence intervals account for this item-level sampling, and they are wider on the smaller subsets. Differences inside these intervals should not be over-interpreted. Benchmark contamination is a further risk for any public benchmark [@sainz2023nlp]. Because the same items were given to every configuration, contamination would shift absolute accuracy, but it would not favor one quantization level or one power mode over another.

Fifth, the output budgets were small for ARC (8 tokens) and BoolQ (4 tokens). Some models continued to write after the answer letter, so a part of the ARC outputs was cut at the budget. Because the answer was emitted within the first tokens, the parse-failure rate stayed low, and the truncation did not change the scored answer. The truncation rate is reported for every configuration, so that this effect can be judged.

Sixth, one serving stack (Ollama 0.33 with its bundled llama.cpp) was used, with the default prompt cache and flash attention enabled. Other engines such as TensorRT-LLM or MLC may give different absolute energies, and faster kernels would shift the whole Pareto front toward lower energy. The ranking between quantization levels and power modes may also depend on how well each kernel is tuned for the Orin GPU.

Finally, the agreement cascade was evaluated offline from measured per-item energies. In a deployed system, the two small models and the large model would have to be resident in memory at the same time, or swapped, and the swap cost was not included. The held-out split protects against selection bias in the choice of the cascade, but the choice was made among a limited set of triples, so a better policy may exist.

Two further limitations concern the scope of the sweeps. The power-mode sweep covers MAXN and the 40 W mode, which share the same GPU power-gating mask and can be switched at run time. The 30 W and 15 W modes change the number of active GPU TPCs and require a reboot on JetPack 6, which could not be scheduled on the shared device. These modes also halve the active GPU and, at 15 W, the online CPU cores. Their effect on latency will therefore be larger than that of the 40 W mode. Whether their lower power can overcome the idle floor remains an open question. A script for these two modes is included in the released code. In the thinking study, the 4B pair compares two post-training variants of one base model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507) rather than one hybrid model with a switch. The reason is that the Ollama tag for the 4B size resolves to a thinking-only model. This serving-stack detail is itself a practical finding. A model whose reasoning cannot be switched off wrote its reasoning into the answer field and failed almost every short-answer item under a small output budget.
