TITLE: Joules per Correct Answer: Energy, Accuracy, and Latency Trade-offs of Quantized Small Language Models across Power Modes on an Edge GPU

ABSTRACT: Small language models are now run on edge devices, where every generated token is paid for from a limited power budget. Most measurements on such devices report tokens per second or tokens per joule, which say nothing about whether the answer is right. In this paper, the energy of a *correct answer* is measured. Twenty-seven configurations of four open-weight model families (Qwen2.5, Llama 3.2, Gemma 2, and Qwen3; 0.5B to 8B parameters) were served through Ollama on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin 32 GB. Quantization levels from Q3_K_M to FP16 were covered. Module power was sampled at 50 Hz from the on-board current sensors and integrated over each of 23,960 requests on ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, and GSM8K. Joules per correct answer (JPCA) is proposed as the primary metric. A six-level quantization ladder, two stock power modes, a thinking on/off switch, and an agreement cascade were evaluated, with bootstrap intervals and paired McNemar tests. JPCA ranged from 2.6 J to 12 J on the short-answer tasks and from 145 J to 791 J on GSM8K. Q4_K_M was the knee of the ladder. Three-bit weights lost 8 to 31 accuracy points, while 5-bit to 16-bit weights gained nothing significant and cost up to 79% more energy per correct answer. Decode energy per token grew linearly with the weight-file size at an effective bandwidth of 164 GB/s, with a fixed cost of 0.35 J per token. A 7B model at Q4_K_M was more accurate than a 3B model at FP16 from a smaller file and was cheaper per correct GSM8K answer. The 40 W power mode left every output unchanged, saved only 1% to 4% of energy, and raised latency by 10%. Thinking multiplied the energy per query by 3 to 225 without a significant accuracy gain for the hybrid models. The agreement cascade was dominated by the best single model, although an oracle router showed a three- to five-fold headroom. Energy measurements were repeatable within a few percent across passes and across 17 days. All per-query logs, power traces, and code are released.

KEYWORDS: edge AI; small language models; energy efficiency; quantization; NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin; power modes; reasoning models; model cascades
