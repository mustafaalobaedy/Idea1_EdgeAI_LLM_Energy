TITLE: Joules per Correct Answer: Energy and Accuracy Trade-offs of Quantized Small Language Models on an Edge GPU

AUTHOR: Mustafa Muwafak Alobaedy | Centre for Image and Vision Computing, Multimedia University, Cyberjaya, Malaysia | Centre for Image and Vision Computing, Multimedia University, 63100 Cyberjaya, Malaysia | mustafa.alobaedy@mmu.edu.my

ABSTRACT: Background. Small language models are now run on edge devices, where every generated token is paid for from a limited power budget. Most measurements on such devices report tokens per second or tokens per joule, which say nothing about whether the answer is right. In this paper, the energy of a correct answer is measured, and the joules per correct answer (JPCA) metric is proposed.
ABSTRACT: Methods. Twenty-seven configurations of four open-weight model families (Qwen2.5, Llama 3.2, Gemma 2, and Qwen3; 0.5 to 8 billion parameters) were served through Ollama on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin 32 GB at quantization levels from 3-bit to 16-bit weights. Module power was sampled at 50 Hz from the on-board current sensors and integrated over each of 23,960 requests on ARC-Challenge, BoolQ, and GSM8K. A six-level quantization ladder, two stock power modes, a reasoning ("thinking") switch, and an agreement cascade were evaluated, with bootstrap confidence intervals and paired McNemar tests on identical items.
ABSTRACT: Results. JPCA ranged from 2.6 J to 12 J on the short-answer tasks and from 145 J to 791 J on GSM8K. The 4-bit level was the knee of the quantization ladder. Three-bit weights lost 8 to 31 accuracy points, while 5-bit to 16-bit weights gained nothing significant and cost up to 79% more energy per correct answer. Decode energy per token grew linearly with the weight-file size at an effective bandwidth of 164 GB/s, with a fixed cost of 0.35 J per token. A 7B model at 4 bits was more accurate than a 3B model at 16 bits from a smaller file and was cheaper per correct GSM8K answer. The 40 W power mode left every output unchanged, saved only 1% to 4% of energy, and raised latency by 10%. Thinking multiplied the energy per query by 3 to 225 without a significant accuracy gain for the hybrid models. The agreement cascade was dominated by the best single model, although an oracle router showed a three- to five-fold headroom. Energy measurements were repeatable within a few percent across passes and across 17 days.
ABSTRACT: Conclusions. For edge deployments of this class, a 3B to 7B model at 4-bit precision, run at the default power mode with thinking disabled, gives the most correct answers per joule. All per-query logs, power traces, and code are released.

KEYWORDS: edge AI; small language models; energy efficiency; quantization; NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin; power modes; reasoning models; model cascades
